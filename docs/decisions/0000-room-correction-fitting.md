# 0000: room-correction fitting deconvolves a Farina sweep, smooths it, and greedily fits at most eight peaking filters that stay inside the room_eq bounds, prefer cuts, never boost past +3 dB and leave nulls alone

  envelope (the `chorus-g12/roomfit` track and the `room_eq` bounds); every default below not
  cited is ASSUMED

## Context

Goal 12's line D asks for room-correction fitting that "produces bounded filters from fixture
recordings". The measurement is a phone's microphone through the control app (K87), which goal
22 builds; this change is what the server does with a recording once it has one. The catalog's
`room_eq` command (at most 8 peaking filters, 20 to 1000 Hz, -12.00 to +3.00 dB, Q 0.5 to 10.0)
and the endpoints' RBJ peaking filters are the other two tracks' (ADRs of `chorus-g12/sound-catalog`
and `chorus-g12/dsp-core`); the fit has to land exactly on what they accept and run.

## Decision

1. **The method is Farina's exponential sine sweep**, deconvolved by linear convolution with
   its inverse filter, because it pushes the loudspeaker's harmonic distortion to before the
   linear response where a window drops it, and its pink spectrum puts energy at the low
   frequencies room correction is about. `Sweep::recommended` is goal 22's starting sweep (5 s,
   10 Hz to 20 kHz, 0.1 s fade-in, stop at the last zero crossing, per Farina 2007).
2. **A recording is refused by name** (`too_short`, `clipped`, `too_quiet`, `too_noisy`) before
   anything is fitted, with the numbers, so the UX can say what to change.
3. **The fit works on the catalog's integer quanta** (whole Hz, centi-dB, milli-Q) and models
   every filter with the crate's own RBJ peaking design and `magnitude_db`, so no rounding sits
   between the fit and the endpoint, and the output is `settings::RoomEqFilter`, the type the
   chain takes. `room_eq_command_json` spells the catalog command.
4. **Bounded by construction**: every coordinate-descent move is checked against the per-filter
   bounds, the combined boost ceiling (+3 dB everywhere from 10 Hz to 20 kHz and at every
   boost's centre), no boost below 100 Hz, and a boost Q that rings out within 0.5 s (REW's
   criterion turned into Q <= 0.2274 f0 / A), before it is taken. The fit never holds an
   out-of-bounds state, so there is nothing to clamp at the end.
5. **Cuts first, nulls alone**: a dip's error weighs half a peak's; a dip reaching 6 dB below the
   target, and the speaker's roll-off below its -3 dB point and half an octave above, are left
   alone (the fit pays for any change there). A cancellation cannot be boosted out, and boosting
   a speaker below its roll-off spends its excursion.
6. **The band is the modal region**, 20 to 300 Hz by default (ASSUMED from the Schroeder
   frequency of typical living rooms, 140 to 200 Hz), the low edge following the speaker's
   extension.
7. **Fixtures are synthetic rooms with ground truth**, regenerated byte for byte from their
   `.params` (the `fixtures/measure` precedent), Rust-only by declaration.

## Every value ASSUMED here

Sweep: fixtures 1 s, 10 Hz to 20 kHz, half full scale, no fade-in; recommended 5 s with a 0.1 s
fade-in. Refusals: response window 0.5 s, clip 3 samples at 0.999 full scale, peak floor -50
dBFS, response-to-noise floor 40 dB (from a simulation, `docs/room-correction.md`). Analysis:
5 ms pre-window, quarter-window fade-out, at least 2^16 FFT points, 1/12-octave rectangular
power smoothing, 48 grid points per octave. Fit: band 20 to 300 Hz, level band 300 Hz to 3 kHz
(median), flat target, extension at -3 dB, half an octave left alone above it, null depth 6 dB,
dip weight 0.5, tolerance 1 dB, minimum filter gain 0.5 dB, 1% improvement per round, two joint
refinement rounds, max combined boost +3 dB, no boost below 100 Hz, boost decay 0.5 s, the
boost check grid 24 per octave from 10 Hz to 20 kHz, filters modelled at 48 kHz. Tests: the
tolerances each test states (1/12 octave and 2.5 dB for a mode, a factor of 4 for the RMS, 0.5%
for the instantaneous frequency, 5% and 1 dB for the impulse).

## Tests (no device, conventions rule 10)

`crates/dsp/tests/roomfit.rs`: byte-for-byte regeneration of every fixture; (a) at most 8
filters inside the bounds and the boost rules, and the command spelling; (b) every synthetic
mode found within 1/12 octave and 2.5 dB; (c) the RMS deviation outside the points left alone
down at least 4 times on the fit's estimate and on the true response, nothing left more than
3 dB above the target, no peak made worse by more than 0.5 dB; (d) nothing above +3.00 dB at a
null or anywhere from 10 Hz to 20 kHz; (e) the four degenerate recordings refused by name; a
configuration outside the bounds refused; cut-only fitting; a target curve moving the fit; the
refusals' wording; the catalog spelling; two worked examples of Farina's paper (the
instantaneous frequency, the delayed impulse) on the fixture sweep and on the recommended one;
and the fitted filters run through the endpoints' f32 biquads on the noiseless room flatten it as
the model predicts. `roomfit/fft.rs`: the FFT against a direct DFT, convolution against a
direct sum.

## Not chosen

- **Spectral division (Kirkeby-regularised) instead of the inverse filter**: equivalent in the
  band, but the inverse filter is the published method a worked example can hold, and its
  output places the distortion visibly before the response.
- **A global optimiser** (all 24 parameters at once): the greedy fit with joint refinement
  reaches about a tenth of the deviation on every fixture; a global search would be harder to
  hold to the bounds at every step and slower to explain.
- **Fitting dB-averaged or unsmoothed responses**: dB averaging weighs nulls beyond their energy;
  unsmoothed responses fit the seat-to-seat ripple.
- **A shared FFT module for the crate**: the visualizer's analysis may want a different shape;
  the transform stays private to `roomfit` until a second user needs it.
- **Boosting dips below 100 Hz**: the phone's own low end is unknown there (CDD +-20 dB).
