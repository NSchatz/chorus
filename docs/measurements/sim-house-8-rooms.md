**THIS IS A SIMULATION AND NOT TIMING EVIDENCE** (BRIEF.md section 3.1 rule 3). Every number below is what the chorus sync model does with the ASSUMED parameters listed here; none was measured on a device, a network or a speaker. It says the servo logic holds BRIEF.md section 2.2's bounds in the model at house scale; whether the real system does is for the measurement rig to say.

## What was run

Two houses, identical except for their network links (a test asserts it): `config/sim-house/8-rooms-switched.house` (switched (P3 option b')) and `config/sim-house/8-rooms-routed.house` (routed (P3 option b)). Each endpoint is one run of the single-client simulator (`crates/sync/src/sim.rs`) with its own crystal, path, jitter stream, wander stream and servo, all against the one server timeline; the server is passive (it only timestamps), so the runs are independent. Seed 20260930, 600 s per run, 10 ms steps, one exchange every 500 ms.

**Inter-device error** for a pair at a true time is the difference of the two endpoints' modelled playout errors (playout position minus the server timeline) at that time. **Settling:** the first 60 s of every run is excluded from every statistic, the same acquisition window the real rig excludes (`sync_hour_settle_seconds = 60`, `config/sync.conf`); every endpoint acquires with one hard resync on its first exchange and none after (asserted). Statistics pool |inter-device error| over every post-settle step (540 s, 10 ms apart) of every pair in the class; p50 and p95 are nearest rank.

Largest single-endpoint |playout error| against the server timeline after settling (what an absolute reference such as video would see): wired 114.1 us switched and 200.2 us routed; Wi-Fi 512.4 us switched and 542.4 us routed.

What it implies for P3, still as a simulation and not timing evidence. The hop's constant delay cancels in the offset estimate. Its constant asymmetry biases every routed endpoint's offset by half of it, the same for every endpoint behind the hop, so it cancels between them: the Wi-Fi / Wi-Fi row, whose link gains only the hop's constant delay and asymmetry, does not move. It would not cancel between a routed endpoint and one that is not routed (a standing offset of half the hop's asymmetry, 30 us here) and it shows against an absolute reference (the single-endpoint peaks above). What does move inter-device error in the model is the hop's QUEUEING on a trunk shared with house traffic: wired cross-room p95 goes from 51.5 us to 103.3 us and max from 138.5 us to 278.2 us, because the heavier queue leaks into the offset of even the least-queued exchange in the window. The routed house is inside all of BRIEF.md section 2.2's acceptable bounds; the same-room classes go above the 0.2 ms aspirational bound (worst same-room max 274.9 us). So the model says the cost of option b is the shared trunk's queueing, which is what option b' removes, and the size of that queueing (a 250 us mean here) is ASSUMED: the routed-versus-L2 measurement in `docs/proposals/P3-speaker-network.md` is still what decides K35.

The cross-room wired / Wi-Fi row is dominated by the Wi-Fi link's ASSUMED 300 us path asymmetry: it biases a Wi-Fi endpoint's offset by 150 us that no wired endpoint shares, which is most of that row's p50. That is the model's reading of why an asymmetric radio path matters more than its jitter, and the parameter WIFI-7 has to measure.

## Models and parameters (every value ASSUMED unless cited)

- Server crystal -7 ppm; endpoint crystals as listed below (spread over BRIEF.md section 6's +/- 20 to 50 ppm); every crystal wanders as a random walk of 0.05 ppm per square root of a second (about 1.2 ppm over the run).
- Servo: the real client's SYNC-4 constants (`config/sync.conf`, asserted equal): kp 0.4, ki 0.08, window 64, smoothing 0.0625, hard resync at 2000 us, clamp 300 ppm.
- Each endpoint's initial epoch offset is uniform in +/- 50 ms from its own stream; its seed is the house seed plus its index through one SplitMix64 step.
- switched link `wired` (wired): base 200 us one way, return minus forward 0 us plus the endpoint's trim, jitter exponential, mean 120 us (capped at 10x).
- switched link `wifi` (wireless): base 1500 us one way, return minus forward 300 us plus the endpoint's trim, jitter burst: quiet exponential mean 400 us; enter 0.03 / exit 0.2 per packet; burst Lomax scale 3000 us, shape 1.5 (capped at 50 ms).
- routed link `wired` (wired): base 240 us one way, return minus forward 60 us plus the endpoint's trim, jitter exponential, mean 250 us (capped at 10x).
- routed link `wifi` (wireless): base 1540 us one way, return minus forward 360 us plus the endpoint's trim, jitter burst: quiet exponential mean 400 us; enter 0.03 / exit 0.2 per packet; burst Lomax scale 3000 us, shape 1.5 (capped at 50 ms).
- Sources: the router hop's "tens of microseconds idle" is the planning research's own ASSUMED figure (https://github.com/NSchatz/chorus/blob/535ed2816b5735153ac81c52a235024aa806f2b5/.claude/goals/2026-09-chorus-research/research-platform-network.md section 4.2); the Wi-Fi burst model is `fixtures/sync/07-wifi-burst.cfg`'s, ASSUMED until WIFI-7's characterization on hardware; the 50 ms burst cap is half the client's 100 ms round-trip admission bound (`max_rtt_us`, `config/sync.conf`). The models are recorded in `docs/decisions/0006-sync-simulator-and-servo.md`.
- Not modelled: DAC, amplifier and acoustic delay; the real client's round-trip admission and staleness rules; packet loss and reordering; the server's own clock wander (common to every endpoint, so it cancels in inter-device error); correlation between endpoints' network delays (each path is independent).
